## 0. In one page

**The players.** נועם, 16 (Brawl Stars, Clash of Clans, Minecraft, Roblox tycoons; a mid-range Android; 5–15-minute sessions on
the bus) and מאיה, 15 (Hay Day, Township, Candy Crush, Subway Surfers, Pinterest; she builds, decorates, collects and shows
friends). Both skip walls of text, waiting with nothing to do, fake choices and "educational" framing. Both love juice, skill,
visible progress, surprise, humour, mastery and something to show off.

**The verdict on v14 (bus/FUN.md, 08:25).** The first 90 seconds are fine; then the player is blocked 84–92% of the time
("חסר ארז … 2:00:00"), half of a fast tapper's taps vanish, 3,598 stones pile up for nothing and a whole wall rises in 40 s of
taps. Noam closes the game at ≈2:00; Maya leaves at 5:19.

**The daughter's loop fixes exactly that — if it is built as a game, not a toll.** A match-3 whose pieces ARE the materials
("ממש את האבנים") turns waiting into playing; the resource meter makes every material visible and spendable; the kings add
decisions and long timers that never block. The spec below keeps her idea whole and adds ten rules:

1. **Never "blocked, wait".** Whatever is short, the pill offers the way to earn it now (a board, a deal, a station).
2. **The first stone in 60 s,** set by the player's own hands, before the first board (an opening gift funds it — ⚑ 1).
3. **One board buys a visible chunk,** 2–4 stone groups; three stars buy a surplus that becomes a choice.
4. **A stone is a stone.** One tap sets one group; a course is a milestone with a number and a sound; never 67% in 10 s.
5. **Earn → spend → tend.** A session alternates the board (3–4 min), building (1–3 min) and management (≤ 1 min).
6. **Key stones by hand:** the crane, the dressing, the plumb and the altar's whole stones — 10–60 s precision games with
   mastery tiers and personal bests.
7. **Every resource has a visible sink,** and the meter says what each is for.
8. **Hard, then easier.** The board's difficulty is a sawtooth; after two losses the next try starts with a special; a loss
   keeps what was gathered.
9. **Something grew while you were away,** and it is yours: crews set stones, a ship arrived, an order is ready.
10. **Our line:** no gambling, loot boxes, purchases, paid lives or moves; no guilt; Shabbat and Yom Tov never break
    anything.

## 2. Research: what the best games in the genre do, and what we take

### 2.1 Match-3 that builds: Gardenscapes, Homescapes, Township (Playrix)

- **Homescapes** (Playrix, 2017; the successor of Gardenscapes, 2016): players solve match-3 puzzles to earn a "Star reward
  that is necessary to complete tasks"; tasks come from a "To-do list", restore the mansion and unlock "new chapters of a
  story". Coins buy power-ups or customise the mansion with "more than 100,000 design patterns"
  ([Wikipedia: Homescapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homescapes); [Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.playrix.homescapes&hl=en_US)).
- **Township**'s core loop is "plant/harvest crops > craft goods > send them away with trains, planes or zoo orders to collect
  coins > upgrade your buildings > expand your territory"; Playrix adds event mini-games "mimicking core mechanics of hit
  casual and hyper-casual games", spaced by two-week gaps "to prevent burnout"
  ([Deconstructor of Fun, 2020](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2020/10/13/how-playrix-township-became-a-billion-dollar-game)).

**What we take.**
- The **to-do list is the construction process.** The board pays for the next step; the step is a task the player chooses.
- The daughter improves on Playrix: their stars are an abstract currency; **our pieces are the material itself.** A stone
  collected on the quarry board flies into the meter and then onto the wall. That link must be *visible* every time (chips
  fly; the next group of stones appears on the site).
- **Choice of décor → choice of order and mark.** Maya cannot repaint a Temple the Mishnah describes, but she can choose
  *which* section rises first, *which* crew works where, and her own **mason's mark** on her stones (mason's marks are real
  Herodian archaeology; CONTENT verifies the example).
- Events every two weeks at most, and never a pay-to-finish.
- Not taken: the Barn storage bottleneck and the Gold Pass (monetisation levers, same article).

### 2.2 Candy Crush (King): difficulty, level design, near-misses, fairness

- **A complexity staircase.** "Newer players early in the game see simpler blockers and more experienced players who've passed
  more levels get to tackle more complicated ones"; new levels mix "recent and long-established blockers"; a new blocker must
  bring "a distinct behaviour or interaction"; designers "often" follow tough stages with lighter ones for "a sense of variation
  and momentum"; bots "simulate large numbers of plays to help us estimate difficulty"
  ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/crafting-candy-crushs-difficulty-blockers-level-design-ai-and-the-complexity-staircase/)).
- **Hard is not the same as unfun.** King measures "time to abandon" and "time to pass" separately; "the longer the level is,
  the less likely it is to be fun"; it prunes the 100 least enjoyable levels for "a very significant uplift in engagement";
  "crazy hard levels never pay off, at least in the long term"; "retention always wins"; and "designing for averages is not a
  good idea" ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)).
- **Near-misses** make players try again, and "near misses activate the same reward systems in the brain as actual gambling
  wins" (a 2009 *Neuron* study); the author notes there is little research on near-misses when skill is involved
  ([Psychology of Games](https://www.psychologyofgames.com/2016/09/the-near-miss-effect-and-game-rewards/); see also
  [Near-miss effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-miss_effect)).

**What we take (for MATCH3).**
- A sawtooth: level n+1 may be easier than level n. Three fields (quarry, forest, gold) give three ladders, so "harder each time"
  (the daughter) holds *per field* while the player can always pick a gentler field.
- Blockers that belong to the site: rubble, roots, earth, a cracked block. The staircase introduces one new blocker per
  3–5 levels and keeps mixing the old ones.
- A level of 3–4 minutes (the owner's "at least three minutes") is long by King's measure, so it must be *rich*: a special by
  0:30, cascades, two goals at most, a clear counter. Our bots (test/playtest) measure "time to pass" and "time to abandon".
- **Near-misses must be true.** A board may never be rigged to "almost" win, and a near-miss is never followed by an offer
  (we have none to make). What we show after a loss: *"חסרו 2 אבנים — מה שאספת נשאר שלך."*
- **Mercy without money.** After two losses on a level, the next try starts with one special on the board. After three, the
  level offers a lighter variant. Retries are free and instant.

### 2.4 Teen psychology, and our ethical line for each lever

- **Self-determination theory.** Autonomy, competence and relatedness in play predict enjoyment and the wish to keep
  playing ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8)).
  → Real choices (field, king, section, crew), visible mastery (tiers, bests), and crews and kings with faces and names.
- **Flow.** Challenge matched to skill, clear goals, immediate feedback; let players adjust the challenge themselves
  ([Chen 2007](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1232743.1232769)). → Three fields as three difficulty ladders; the precision
  games' levels follow the player's tier, not a fixed ramp.
- **The goal gradient.** Effort speeds up as the goal nears ([Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)).
  → Every bar shows the *remaining* count: "עוד 2 אבנים לנדבך", "עוד 3 לגובה הנדבך הבא".
- **Endowed progress.** A card with 2 of 10 stamps pre-filled is completed more often than an empty card of 8
  ([Nunes & Drèze 2006](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=991962)). → The opening gift, and a course bar
  that starts with the master mason's cornerstone already set.
- **Zeigarnik, really Ovsiankina.** An interrupted task pulls us back to finish it; the memory effect replicates poorly
  (docs/HOOKS.md §2.7). → End every session on an *open, pleasant* loop (a course at 9/12, a ship at sea), never on a
  threat.
- **Variable rewards without gambling.** Loot boxes are linked to problem gambling in adults
  ([Zendle & Cairns 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0206767)) and in 16–18-year-olds
  ([Zendle, Meyer & Over 2019](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/6/6/190049/94826/Adolescents-and-loot-boxes-links-with-problem));
  the UK Children's Code forbids nudge techniques that lead children to give up data or weaken their privacy
  ([ICO, standard 13](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques/)),
  and we hold play to the same spirit.
  → Surprise comes only from **earned, deterministic** moments: a find appears on the Nth perfect joint, a king's gift on the
  first deal; the album shows every item and *how* it is found. No roll, no chest, no price.
- **Loss aversion without guilt.** Players feel losses more than gains; games exploit it with withering crops and broken
  streaks ([PocketGamer.biz on wither mechanics](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-f2p-designers-should-balance-wither-and-daily-rewards/);
  [Zagal et al., dark patterns](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~zagal/Papers/Zagal_et_al_DarkPatterns.pdf)).
  → Nothing decays, expires or is taken. A lost board keeps what was gathered; an unfinished order just rolls; offline progress
  is capped but never lost.
- **Peers.** Adolescents' reward circuitry responds more strongly when peers are watching
  ([Chein et al. 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2010.01035.x)).
  → Something to *show*: a dusk photo of "my Temple", a personal-best card, the album. Local only, no public ranking, no
  pressure to recruit friends.
- **Juice.** Small, immediate, physical feedback makes the same rules feel alive
  ([Jonasson & Purho, "Juice it or lose it"](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/juice-it-or-lose)). → Every contact has a
  sound at the frame of contact, a haptic tick and a number pop (src/game/audio.js).

## 3. The fun spec of the new Phase A

Terms. **Step** = one process step of one section of a stage (survey · dig · quarry · dress · lift & set a course · inspect ·
finish). **Group** = the stones one tap sets (3–6 stones; a crew sets them while the hero works the key stone). **Course** =
one layer of one section. **Key stone** = a cornerstone, lintel, great stone or altar stone: set by hand in a precision game.

### 3.3 Day 1 (three sessions: the bus, the evening, before sleep)

- **Session start (≤ 5 s to a meaningful action).** A short "בזמן שלא היית" line, *shown on the site*: the crew set 14 stones
  (they appear), the ship is halfway, one order is ready to claim. Then the pill's next action.
- **Session shape.** Earn → spend → tend, 2–3 times. The bus session: one board and one course. The evening: three boards, a
  key stone, a king, the orders.
- **Session end.** An open, pleasant loop: "הנדבך 9/12", "הספינה מגיעה בערב". No threat, no countdown to a loss.
- **End of day 1:** the Mount levelled (the har stage, re-shaped as courses of the platform's retaining wall) and the azara
  wall's first courses; the album 2–3 of 8; crew level 2 in one trade.

### 3.4 Week 1

- Stages: har → wall → the first gate. The first **great stone** (a crane "boss": heavier, windier, a longer rope).
- **Daily challenge** on six days (Shabbat and Yom Tov: "יום מנוחה", nothing lost, the crews rest too).
- The weekly goal (exists: week.js) re-pointed at the new loop: "3 נדבכים · 2 עסקאות · 5 אבני פינה".
- The album's first full set (a small ceremony and a real-archaeology card).
- Noam has a personal best on the crane he wants to beat; Maya has "her" gate, with her mark on its stones.

### 3.5 The long arc (weeks)

Chapters follow the stages: the Mount → the walls → the gates → the altar (the whole-stones game, Middot 3:4) → the kiyor (the
water flows; WATER) → the Ulam → the Heichal → the Kodesh HaKodashim (built and seen, never entered) → Chanukat HaBayit →
Phase B. Each chapter adds one board blocker, one precision variant and one king. The whole Temple takes weeks for a regular
player (the owner: "שייקח לו זמן לבנות את הבית"), with no hard wall and never money. Mastery keeps its value after the
end: tiers, bests, the album and the golden vine stay.

### 3.6 The rhythm between the board, the building, the precision games and the kings

**The session grammar: EARN → SPEND → TEND.**

| block | length | what the player does | rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| EARN — the board | 3–4 min | a match-3 level in the field that matches the next step's shortfall | one level buys 2–4 groups (a quarter to half a course); 3 stars → a surplus |
| SPEND — the site | 1–3 min | taps set groups; the course bar climbs; a course is a milestone | every tap is answered; a tap during a stroke is queued, never swallowed |
| KEY — precision | 10–60 s | the crane, the dressing, the plumb, the whole stones | at most one per 2–3 groups; only on key stones |
| TEND — management | ≤ 1 min | orders, crews, kings, the album | never blocks; never a wall of text |

- **The next action is always one tap away** and always an action, never a timer. The waiting option (a ship, a crew) is shown
  *under* it.
- **The board never runs twice in a row by force.** After a board, the player spends; he may choose another board.
- **The kings** open when a shortfall is cedar, gold or bronze (the long materials) and when a ship arrives.
- **Pacing by the clock.** Noam's bus session (5 min) must contain one EARN and one SPEND; Maya's evening (30 min) must not
  feel repetitive: the board's field changes, the precision game changes, the orders change.

### 3.7 The precision games of the site (FUN builds them: src/game/minigames/)

| kind | name | length | the skill | tiers | the true fact (CONTENT verifies) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `crane` | המנוף | 15–30 s | a stone swings on the treadwheel's rope; tap to lower it onto its bed; its joints must not fall over the joints of the course below | שוליה · אומן · רב אומן · מופת | Herodian courses were laid without mortar, each joint offset from the joint below (archaeology of the Western Wall; SOURCES.md) |
| `dress` | הסיתות | 20–40 s | tap in rhythm along the four margins (the chisel), then the boss; the stone changes from rough to drafted to dressed | same | the drafted margin and flat boss of Herodian ashlars (SOURCES.md) |
| `plumb` | האנך והפלס | 10–20 s | stop the swinging plumb bob at true vertical, then centre the level | same | the builders' plumb line (SOURCES.md) |
| `whole` | אבנים שלמות | 20–40 s | inspect field stones from Beit Kerem: accept the whole ones, set aside a nicked stone or one touched by iron | same | Middot 3:4: the altar's stones are whole, iron invalidates by touch, a nick invalidates (SOURCES.md) |

Each game: one fact line at the end (not before), a personal best, a tier, a calm "אפשר לדלג" after 20 s without progress, a
demo autopilot, and a DOM fallback when the 3D site API is missing.

## 6. ⚑ Decisions for the owner (one line each, with a default)

1. ⚑ **המשחק הראשון לפני האבן הראשונה?** ברירת מחדל: מתנת פתיחה מממנת את האבן הראשונה (תוך 60 שניות), והמשחק הראשון (3 דקות) נפתח מיד אחריה.
2. ⚑ **כמה בנייה קונה שלב אחד?** ברירת מחדל: 2–4 קבוצות אבנים (רבע עד חצי נדבך), ושלושה כוכבים נותנים עודף.
3. ⚑ **קושי "עולה ממשחק למשחק"?** ברירת מחדל: עולה בכל שדה בנפרד, בשיני מסור (קשה ואחריו קל), ואחרי שני הפסדים עזרה חינם.

## 9. Wave 8B (28.9, s8-games): every mini-game playable by hand on a phone

The owner played v16 on a phone: the in-between games "run at speed and cannot be played", the quarry "plays by itself",
the dressing and the cornerstone games "are broken". Played again by real touch at 390×844 (no autoplay flag), the causes:

| what the owner saw | the cause (v16) | the fix (s8-games) |
|---|---|---|
| a game "runs at speed" and ignores the finger | a stale `#demo` (FLOW8B's trap) sends `ctx.demo`: the autopilot plays at ×1.5 and the board ignores input (`onInput: if (!demo)`) | a real touch (a trusted pointer) takes the game or the board over at once, at ×1; the autopilot stops (frame.js `human`, match3 `onTouch`) |
| the quarry "plays by itself" | the crew acted after 5 s without a tap ("החוצבים העמיקו") and finished the wedges after 17 s; the wedges drained 0.035/s from the start | a player's game is *patient* (`M.patient`): nothing is done for the player; after 7 s idle the hand comes back, after 20 s "אפשר לדלג". Level 0: 3 blows a wedge, a slow drain that starts only with the first blow |
| "I don't understand what to tap" | one long hint line; nothing on the target | the first-time how-to: one line over the canvas, the hand on the target, "הקש כדי להתחיל" — the game waits. Each part's how-to too (the wedges, the boss, the level) |
| the timing is invisible | the right moment was shown only by a thin bar | the hand (`M.cue()` → where, and `now`) presses at the right moment through the first round; the dressing's next mark has an approach ring that closes on the beat, and a missed mark stays rough |
| the dressing is too fast and long | 24 marks at 0.62 s, windows ±0.15 s | level 0: 16 marks at 0.8 s, windows ±0.18 s; then +1 mark a side and −0.06 s a level |
| the first try is as hard as the next | — | a slow first round: the first stone, cut and check swing ×1.3 slower (levels 0–1); the whole stones: 3 s a stone (was 2.3), the first two wait for the player |
| the fact never changes | a fixed fact per game | FLOW8B's `nextFact(topic)` (ctx or `__BH_FACTS`, guarded; FACTS8B's topics: stone for the crane, the dressing and the quarry, herod for the plumb, yesod for the whole stones); the first try may use FLOW's `ctx.fact`; the game's own fact is the fallback |

**Contracts.** Unchanged: `runMinigame(kind, ctx)` → the same result. New, all optional: a game's `howto`, `guide`, `howAt`,
`topic`; a model's `cue()` → `{ x, y, now, timed }` or `{ btn }` and `patient`; `ctx.howto` (true: always, false: never);
the how-to is remembered per device (`localStorage['bh-mg-howto']`, guarded; a private window shows it once a session).
Events for MUSIC8B: `minigameStart {kind, level}`, `mgBeat {beat: split | land}`, `mgHuman` / `m3human`; `sfx.context` (read-only).
Test hooks: `.fx-mg`'s `__mg.cue()` / `__mg.state()` and the board's `view.locked` — a bot reads what a player sees and taps
with a real finger. Node `simulate()` keeps the old non-patient models (a player who never taps still ends, for the bots).

**Proof by touch** (test/minigames-touch.cjs, one browser, 390×844, `hasTouch`, `page.touchscreen.tap` and CDP touch drags):
each game shows its how-to and waits; nothing moves on (7–9.5 s untouched); each is won by touch following the hand
(three runs on a loaded 2-core machine: crane 93–97 %, great stone 97–100 %, dressing 70–95 %, quarry 100 %, plumb 75–90 %,
whole stones 100 %); #demo hands a game and a board over at the first touch; the board swaps by drag and by tap-tap (6/6), and
a field's first board shows the swipe with a hand. The simulated players (test/playtest/minigames-calib.mjs) keep the curve:
level 0 is רב אומן–מופת for both, level 4 still spreads over all four tiers for the slower one.
